August ?? - ???: I will then amplify the V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene, which is often used to identify the composition of species within the microbial community. This will be done using PCR, performed in triplicate for each sample sequenced. Once the rounds of PCR are completed, I will verify the presence of barcoded amplicons via gel electrophoresis and pool them in equimolar ratios. After this, the amplicons are then sent out for sequencing by the Smithsonian LAB. Sequencing is performed via 250bp paired-end reads on the Illumina platform.

After the sequences are returned to us, I will process the sequence data using the DADA2 pipeline.. This begins with a quality assessment, followed by filtering and trimming away the low quality reads. The paired-end reads are then merged to get our full sequences. I will perform a secondary quality check, inspecting the amount of reads that made it through each step of this process to check for any possible anomalies. Taxonomy will then be assigned to our reads with the use of kraken2, allowing us to determine the composition of the bacterial population.

The Far Future: After QA/QC and identification of the composition of the microbiome, I will begin the data analysis. First, I will run statistical tests to determine the alpha and beta diversity of each sample, where alpha diversity is diversity within a sample, while beta diversity is diversity between samples. During this initial analysis of the experiment, we are focusing mainly on the O. faveolata specimens, where the probiotic treatment was most effective in halting disease progression. First, I will investigate how the microbial community diversity is affected by probiotic treatment and SCTLD exposure. At this point I will assess possible relationships between specific bacterial species/strains and disease resilience or susceptibility by comparing bacterial compositions with disease events (or lack thereof) from the initial experiment. Once results are finalized I will write the report.
